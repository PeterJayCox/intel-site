The Intelligence Cycle

cycleanalysiscollectiondissemination
Open Source

The foundational model of the intelligence process: a structured loop for converting raw information into decisions. The classic doctrine formulation is Direction – Collection – Processing/Analysis – Dissemination, extended with feedback.

Six phases (applied framing)

  1. Collection — gathering relevant data, facts and information.
  2. Analysis — examining collected data to find patterns, trends and insights.
  3. Synthesis — organising and interpreting the analysed information into coherent conclusions.
  4. Evaluation — assessing accuracy, reliability and usefulness of conclusions.
  5. Application / Next steps — using results to inform decisions or action.
  6. Review & closure — documenting what was learned and reflecting on effectiveness.

Notes from the profession

  • The AIPIO 25 conference reinforced that the cycle is a core competency of tradecraft, and that incremental improvement across each stage compounds ("aggregation of marginal gains").
  • Alone, intelligence has no value — it must be relevant, timely and useful to the decision-maker.
  • The cycle is a simplification; modern practice augments it with structured analytic techniques and continuous feedback.
