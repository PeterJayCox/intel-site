Estimative Language

disseminationwritinganalysis
Open Source

A standardised vocabulary for expressing likelihood and confidence in analytical products, so judgements are calibrated and consistently understood (Sherman Kent tradition, adapted for cyber).

Likelihood / probability scale (future or uncertain events)

Phrase Range
Almost certain ≈ 95–99%
Very likely ≈ 80–95%
Likely ≈ 60–80%
Roughly even chance ≈ 40–60%
Unlikely ≈ 20–40%
Highly unlikely / Remote ≈ 1–20%

Confidence in the judgement itself

  • High confidence — multiple high-quality, independent sources; strong corroboration; limited alternative explanations.
  • Moderate confidence — credible sources but some gaps, single-source dependency, or plausible alternatives.
  • Low confidence — limited, fragmented, or low-credibility information; significant uncertainty remains.

Usage rules

  • State confidence explicitly with every major judgment.
  • Example: "We assess with moderate confidence that Actor X is preparing a new phishing campaign targeting the financial sector."
  • Avoid absolute statements when evidence is incomplete; avoid vague intensifiers ("very sophisticated") without support.
  • Prefer "we assess / we judge / evidence indicates" over "we believe".

This vocabulary pairs with the wiki's confidence frontmatter field — the page-level analogue of per-judgment confidence. See also Admiralty Rating for source-level grading.
