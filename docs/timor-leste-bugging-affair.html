Timor-Leste Bugging Affair

casehumintjurisdictionoversight
Open Source

ASIS's 2004 operation against Timorese cabinet deliberations during maritime boundary negotiations — and the subsequent prosecutions of whistleblower "Witness K" and lawyer Bernard Collaery. It remains the sharpest Australian test case in debates about whistleblowing, secrecy in courts, and the limits of Intelligence Oversight.

Subject, dates and scope

  • 2004 — ASIS operation targeting Timor-Leste cabinet deliberations during negotiations over the maritime boundary
  • Prosecutions — of "Witness K" (the source) and Bernard Collaery (his lawyer) under national security secrecy laws
  • Debates engaged — whistleblowing, closed-court secrecy, and whether the oversight architecture (Intelligence Oversight) has limits

Key findings and confidence

  • The affair is a documented case of intelligence collection against a negotiating counterpart — public record from court proceedings and reporting. Confidence: high on the events; the policy debate around it remains contested.

Linkage

  • Intelligence Oversight — the limits the affair exposed
  • National Intelligence Community — ASIS sits within the NIC's collection agencies
