Groupthink

analysisbias
Open Source

(Janis 1972) A mode of thinking in cohesive groups in which the drive for unanimity overrides a realistic appraisal of alternative courses of action. It is the social analogue of individual bias — consensus pressure degrading the quality of analytic judgement in teams.

Symptoms

  • Illusion of invulnerability — collective overconfidence and risk-taking.
  • Collective rationalisation — dismissing warnings that contradict the shared view.
  • Self-censorship and pressure on dissenters; "mindguards" who shield the group from disconfirming information.
  • Illusion of unanimity — silence read as agreement.

In intelligence analysis

  • Analytic teams converge on an estimate without genuine challenge; dissent is suppressed or self-censored.
  • Reinforces Confirmation Bias at the group level — the group confirms a shared view rather than testing it.

Countermeasure

  • Devil's Advocacy and red-teaming: appoint someone to argue against the prevailing judgement.
  • Structured techniques (ACH) and parallel/anonymous assessment so dissent is not personally costly.
  • Deliberately invite outside perspectives before the group converges.
