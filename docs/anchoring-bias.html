Anchoring Bias

analysisbias
Open Source

Over-reliance on the first piece of information offered (the anchor) when forming subsequent estimates, with insufficient adjustment away from it — even when the anchor is arbitrary or irrelevant.

In intelligence analysis

  • An early estimate, a premature headline figure, or a source's first claim can anchor the final judgement.
  • Once an anchor is set, subsequent reporting is assimilated toward it rather than independently appraised.
  • Anchoring interacts with Overconfidence Bias: the anchored figure is then held with unwarranted certainty.

Countermeasure

  • Key Assumptions Check to surface the initial figure as an assumption rather than a fact.
  • Generate independent estimates before seeing others', then reconcile (avoids inheriting the anchor).
