Intelligence Relationships in a Contested Region

jurisdictionalliancecollection
Open Source

For Australia, collection and liaison policy are now foreign policy. The National Intelligence Community runs an alliance system built on Five Eyes while managing a web of partners outside it, against a region increasingly contested by China.

China as the organising problem

Espionage and foreign interference have been ASIO's declared principal security concern since the late 2010s, even as the terrorism threat level was raised to probable in August 2024 and politically motivated violence (including among the very young) returned to the foreground. Those are not contradictions — the service runs both. The analytic and diplomatic problem is speaking plainly about PRC intelligence activity (the "A-team" and related units Burgess has described publicly; interference in politics, campuses and diaspora communities) without sliding into collective suspicion of Chinese-Australians. The 2018 Espionage and Foreign Interference Act and the Foreign Influence Transparency Scheme are the legislative spine; their effectiveness is still being argued.

Partners who are not Five Eyes

  • Japan — closest extra-Five Eyes partner; the relationship is outrunning the formal architecture.
  • Singapore — highly capable, discreet, central to Southeast Asian liaison.
  • Indonesia — indispensable and historically bruised (East Timor, people-smuggling, Papua, occasional military-intelligence friction); political sensitivity caps how far integration can go.
  • India / Quad — growing, constrained by trust, capacity, India's Russia relationship, and New Delhi's instinct against entangling intelligence alliances.
  • France — a serious Indo-Pacific intelligence and military actor; the 2021 submarine cancellation poisoned the well, subsequently repaired but not forgotten.
  • Pacific island states — tiny indigenous collection capacity, real PRC intelligence and political-influence activity, and Australian (plus NZ, US, French) capacity-building via AFP, ASD/ACSC and liaison. The 2022 Solomon Islands–China security pact was a shock that intelligence warning and diplomatic machinery are still digesting.

The sharing dilemma

Assessments only matter if they reach people who can act. Sharing with Jakarta, Tokyo or Suva means accepting different classification cultures, leak risk, and sometimes penetration risk. ONI products written for the National Security Committee are not the same artefacts as sanitised briefs for a partner service (National Intelligence Community). A small community feels this more than a large one: every liaison officer is also an opportunity cost against all-source effort.

Alliance dependence

Pine Gap and related facilities remain the physical core of Australia's value to US intelligence (Five Eyes). Strategic debate in Canberra — Hugh White and others on one side; alliance orthodoxy on the other — includes what those facilities mean in a US–China war, and how much independent assessment Australia retains if collection and targeting are fused. Gyngell's "fear of abandonment" still describes the political psychology; AUKUS has intensified it rather than resolved it.

New Zealand and Canada

The awkward near-abroad of the Anglosphere: still inside the inner intelligence world, increasingly outside the inner defence-technology world under AUKUS. Managing that without rupturing UKUSA is a live, quiet task (AUKUS Intelligence Sharing).
