Volume Mistaken for Development

analysisbiasstructural
Open Source
Signature in a draft Raise, do not fix alone

The reporting curve is rising and the product reads it as the activity curve.

The one move

Separate the reporting curve from the activity curve, and say which one the trend rests on.

A structural bias, and the one most often mistaken for a finding. Reporting volume is a property of the collection system; activity is a property of the target. When the two are read as one line, a quarterly collection decision looks like an escalation.

How it bites

  • The trend belongs to us. A new feed, a re-tasked sensor, a partner deciding to share, a change in a detection threshold — each raises the curve without anything changing on the ground.

  • It reads as corroboration. More reports feel like more evidence; in fact the same fact can generate many reports, and one fact reported ten times is still one fact.

  • It scales with attention. The subject that gets looked at gets reported, which gets looked at harder. The loop is invisible in a product that shows only volume.

  • It compounds with Recency Bias and Availability Heuristic: the recent, the plentiful and the easily recalled all point the same way.

Countermeasure — and why it is not an analyst’s to fix

The analyst’s move: two lines, labelled — reporting volume, and activity on the ground. If only one series exists, say so, and say the trend is a statement about collection.

The structural fix needs the collection manager: a record of tasking changes, threshold changes and partner-sharing decisions, so that volume can be adjusted rather than interpreted. See Structured Analytic Techniques for the baseline discipline, and Insensitivity to Sample Size for the counting error that usually accompanies this one.
