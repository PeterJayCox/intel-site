Hindsight Bias

analysisbias
Open Source

After an outcome is known, the tendency to over-estimate how foreseeable it was beforehand — the "I knew it all along" effect, also called creeping determinism. Memory of one's prior judgements is rewritten to fit the result.

In intelligence analysis

  • Post-mortems conclude a failure "should have been seen", discouraging honest appraisal of what was actually knowable at the time.
  • Erodes the ability to learn from a track record, because past uncertainty is systematically forgotten.
  • Feeds Overconfidence Bias in future work ("we were basically right last time").

Countermeasure

  • Keep written prior judgements with explicit Estimative Language, and score them against outcomes — so hindsight cannot rewrite what was actually said.
  • Structured lessons-learned that distinguish "foreseeable at the time" from "clear only in retrospect".
